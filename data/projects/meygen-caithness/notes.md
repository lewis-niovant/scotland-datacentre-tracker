# MeyGen data centre, Caithness — research notes (2026-07-30)

## Chain of evidence
1. Nov 2019: SIMEC Atlantis Energy (AIM: SAE) announced plans for the "world's first ocean-powered data centre" in Caithness, supplied by private wire from an ~80 MW expansion of the MeyGen tidal array (seabed lease and consents held), within a "virtual power plant" with nearby wind farms; hyperscale ambition, international subsea fibre links envisaged, target live 2024. Early-2020 reporting claimed corporate deals with "major IT companies" were weeks away — none ever materialised publicly.
2. Mar 2020: Highland Council EIA screening request 20/01258/SCRE, "Development of data centre", Land 500m West of Philips Mains, Mey (decided 2020-03-25). Location and timing strongly indicate this is the SAE proposal, but the applicant name is unconfirmed — Highland's WAM portal returned HTTP 503 on the retrieval date, so details come from the PlanIt aggregate record.
3. 2020-2026: no PAN, scoping or application for the data centre. MeyGen Phase 1 (6 MW, 4 turbines) continues operating and Phase 2 has secured 59 MW of CfDs, but SAE's onshore planning activity at Mey is now the 300 MW / 1.2 GWh Mey BESS (s36) on 10.7 ha next to the approved Gills Bay 132kV substation.
4. 2026: Invest Highland still markets "MeyGen" as a 20+ ha greenfield hyperscale data centre opportunity owned by SAE, availability "short term", citing tidal generation as "more reliable, predictable power". No fibre or grid detail given.

## Judgement calls
- Status "strategic_opportunity" (current reality: marketed opportunity, dormant proposal), maturity M1 because the 2020 screening evidences a real early-development step beyond pure marketing. Verification "verified" on the strength of the planning record; overall confidence low given the six-year gap and unconfirmed applicant.
- The 80 MW figure is recorded as onsite_generation (tidal supply), not data centre load — no load figure was ever disclosed.
